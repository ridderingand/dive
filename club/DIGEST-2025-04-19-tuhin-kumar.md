## Takeaways (Granola)

From Granola Editing Notes "Editing Tuhin Kumar" (2025-04-17) Takeaways (primary gold):

- "Things that are really well designed do have an art like quality" — design function exists because we needed a function to fight for the user.
- How designs go beyond JTBD — "Of course that's the job to be done. But what can we bring to the table…" — otherwise the moment a better model comes you switch.
- Can't think of AI as just a tool — another entity is there; chat resists calling it a tool, but at a deep subconscious level you can't.
- Impact of working with a research team — first time design has met a function even more timeline-averse than design; not normal engineering; get discoveries out ASAP; often you can shape what research should research.
- Gestures 10 years ago ≈ AI today — "It wasn't until we got the slide to unlock that we realized…"
- Solutions in search of problems — used to see problem + tools and solve; now also assume the arc of technology.
- Compounding is very real in AI — feedback cycle puts you two months sooner next update.
- Playing with AI discovers features — "you wouldn't likely think of brainstorm as a feature, but when you're in the medium…"
- AI as a new material — before UI design was graphic design…
- Design process more spread out now — weeks of pure iteration rarer; work spreads across research phases.
- Skills for an AI world — great visual design is still baseline / gets you in the door; amplifiers on top; curiosity + growth mindset for hires.
- Feedback: role doesn't matter — have you thought deeply enough? Sign of respect.
- Craft-oriented companies = people who care more (not a process thing).
- Art of writing system prompts — GPT-3.5 special-case rules put a ceiling on intelligence; don't over-engineer prompts as models improve.
