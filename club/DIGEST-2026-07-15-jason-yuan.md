## Summary

Jason Yuan (New Computer / Dot → Hivemind) screenshares the multi-year creative arc behind Dot and Hivemind: memory and guides explorations that never shipped, six months of visual systems deleted down to Chronicles, Dot 2.0 mood boards that look like art not interface, Lore Keeper social experiments instead of traditional user research, and a Hivemind visual identity built from taboo, occult, birds, and punctuation before a single logo was locked. Core claims: every shipped thing is the distillation of 99 nos; create the world (music, cinematic imagery) before you know what the product is; theater trains you to hunt human truth; users don't know what they want — run experiments that let them tell you without telling you; taste is editing with a point of view, not adding; Hivemind is the most honest work he has made because the motivation was "what I believe is true," not "what the world wants." Why it matters: rare end-to-end BTS from someone who treats product design as art direction and world-building — and who will kill six months of beautiful work when the tech cannot make it great.

## Takeaways (Granola)

- 99 nos for every yes. Jason spent 6 months doing visual work around memories and guides, including building a system to create infinite illustrations, and he deleted all of it.
- He creates the world before he even knows what the product is (ex: music for the video, or generated imagery).
- Jason’s creative process is rooted in theater, not design.
- Why Jason doesn’t really believe in user research.
- Taste is editing with a point of view, not adding.
- The Hivemind visual identity came from the most unexpected sources.
- The design industry has lost its artistry, and Jason finds that concerning.
- Hivemind is the most honest work Jason has ever made, and that distinction matters to him.
