## Summary

Charlie Deets (The Browser Company; previously Human Interface at Apple on Safari, also WhatsApp) walks Ridd through what designing browsers is actually like across three products with opposite positioning: Safari as ubiquitous invisible utility, Arc as overflowing playful experiment, and Dia as a defined "internet computer" with an easier on-ramp and complexity introduced over time. Core claims: browser design is a gradient of use cases (tabs, windows, profiles) where feedback and fast internal builds beat pure data; Charlie's personal triad is simple + practical + high craft, which creates confidence; in a world where everyone can execute with AI, his special sauce is reducing and clarifying designs for engineers rather than coding production himself — so at work he has shifted *toward* Figma and *away* from AI prototyping (while Claude Code is personal downtime); Dia spends its novelty budget on new AI capabilities (morning brief as effortless context) while shipping the obvious Chrome-familiar UI (bookmarks bar) after months of failed clever alternatives; craft lives in nearly invisible moments (URL bar shows title+domain, copy-without-trackers, button micro-interactions, new-tab prism, split-tab education). Why it matters: a rare Safari→Arc→Dia craft tour plus a contrarian 2026 take — more Figma, less AI prototyping — grounded in principles, decision logging, and presentation tactics.
