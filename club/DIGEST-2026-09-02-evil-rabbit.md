**Granola summary Takeaways (generated; no Ridd-typed Takeaways in Editing note)**

- Ivo started designing at 9, had paying clients at 12, and was leading a design team at 21
  - "I was 21. I was super young. Yeah, yeah, that was crazy."
  - His first clients were racing car drivers in Argentina. He built their websites in Flash and Dreamweaver.
  - By 21 he was at Viacom (MTV, Nickelodeon) and was quickly asked to run the design team.
  - "The director came to me, he's like, man, I think you should run the design team. Like, people look at you, they love what you're doing."
  - His only prior management experience was running his own freelance operation.
  - His father's stroke forced him to leave university and lean fully into design as both livelihood and escape.
  - "I found in design a place where I could escape, create beautiful things."
- The Popcorn Time redesign happened in a single Friday night and blew up overnight
  - He met the creator at a Google event in Buenos Aires and messaged him: "Today when you come back from MTV, I'm gonna redesign your app."
  - The creator's first response: "Oh man, I'm scared."
  - Ivo's key move: "I killed the sidebar. I make it horizontal."
  - He posted the designs to a UI discussion thread and woke up to 2,000 messages.
  - The original creator was not happy: "This is not a democracy."
  - Within a week the creator asked for the Photoshop files and implemented the design.
  - The project was shut down shortly after due to cease and desist letters, but the design lived on through countless forks.
  - "Popcorn Time was meant for countries where the movies were not there on time."
  - Ivo is careful to note: "I just provided a design. I didn't connect anything, any library or anything."
- Zeit's naming system was intentional and deeply connected: Zeit (time), Now, the delta triangle, Next
  - "The original name was Zeit, which means time in German. The very first product was Now, and it was the delta triangle, and the main framework was Next. So the three things were connected; they were part of the branding from day one."
  - Losing the Zeit name was hard for Ivo. That's part of why the Geist font exists.
  - "That's why Geist, our main font, preserves part of that core essence."
  - The triangle was designed to stand alone without the wordmark from early on.
  - "You don't need to read Vercel to actually feel Vercel."
  - The new Vercel website launched with only the triangle and no wordmark. Ivo describes it as his dream.
  - "I go to a bus stop and I see a billboard and it's using Geist. For me, that is incredible."
- Guillermo taught Ivo how to say no, and that shaped how he operates
  - "One of the hardest things is actually to say no to these incredible ideas."
  - "I learned from Guillermo how to sometimes say, hey, no, we love this, we spent so much time on it, but unfortunately we need to shift."
  - Ivo describes Guillermo as his mentor from the Auth0 days onward.
  - "I know for sure that I'm gonna end up doing something, I don't know what yet, but something with this guy."
  - Guillermo flew to Argentina to meet Ivo after he said he was leaving Auth0.
- The Vercel brand succeeded by being minimal, neutral, and transparent
  - "When you create a product that feels neutral enough, it is transparent, it's there, it helps you to go from zero to one, but you don't see it. It means that you did a great job."
  - The very first Vercel website was brutalist: monospace font, terminal aesthetic, black and white.
  - The first billboard was a terminal command to install their AI package. "People went mad."
  - Other companies started copying the minimal black and white billboard aesthetic.
  - "The things that we did right was keeping everything super, super minimal, simple and easy to digest in every single thing that we put out there."
  - Ivo started tweeting a triangle with different textures daily to build brand recognition before the product was widely known.
- Ivo's creative team shifted from making static assets to building internal tools
  - "Originally, we were building the same things that everyone else was building. OG images for announcements, tiny videos for a story or a tweet."
  - Now the team builds tools so other teams can generate their own assets without a designer.
  - Example: a profile photo generator that applies the Vercel aesthetic automatically.
  - Example: a billboard system that adapts one graphic to any spec or location.
  - Example: teaching a marketing person motion design principles so they can direct an agent to build videos themselves.
  - "It's actually allowing people to upscale themselves 10, 20 times, moving super quick versus a few years ago where the creative team was literally a bottleneck."
  - "We're not just creating ad hoc or standalone assets or solutions. We're actually improving their daily workflow."
- Ivo deliberately avoids the chief design officer title because he wants to keep learning
  - "A lot of people ask me, Ivo, why are you not the chief design officer? Because I want to have more people above myself that I can learn from."
  - He now reports to both the CPO (Tomo) and the CMO (Keith), which he calls a superpower.
  - "I know a little bit of product and a little bit of marketing, and I can combine those things and create the best out of it."
  - He moved the creative/brand team under marketing while maintaining a vertical connection to product.
- Ivo doesn't like hiring stars because they're hard to integrate into a team culture
  - "One of the things that I don't love doing is hiring stars because stars have their… it's hard to give them feedback. It's hard to add them and make them part of a bigger machine."
  - Vercel's values include "same team," which Ivo takes seriously.
  - His preferred approach: let people bring their own magic, then give feedback after.
  - "We let them cook. We let them bring their knowledge."
  - Example: Luis came from Pentagram, immediately wanted brand guidelines. Ivo reframed it as an opportunity: "This is your chance to own this project."
  - Example: Rauno brought interaction and animation knowledge and applied it across the dashboard, blog, and video announcements.
  - "Cutting their wings or not letting them fly enough or high enough is not a good thing."
- Ivo sees the AI era as a rebirth for creative people, similar to how Flash felt as a kid
  - "Don't you feel that it's similar to when Flash was released back in the day? I had the same feeling. I was like, mom, dad, I have superpowers."
  - "We are not just a designer anymore. We are builders. We can create whatever we want, wherever we want."
  - "Remember when I tweeted this and I said, I'm just a designer and people went mad?"
  - He helped build FX, Vercel's CLI coding agent, and designed its landing page as a callback to the Zeit era.
  - "The craziest ideas that you had or you have, you can build them."
  - He describes the limit now as the tokens and the money, and sometimes ourselves.
- Ivo's proudest legacy at Vercel is the brand keepers he created across the whole company
  - "To be in a room seeing other people defending… hey, I would make this more minimal. And it's not me saying it after all these years."
  - Non-designers, including salespeople, now say things like "this doesn't feel Vercel" or "too much color."
  - "I am in the DNA of this company. I didn't do it because of that. It was natural and organic, but it feels great."
  - When he joined, only Ivo dressed in black. Over time the whole company shifted toward that aesthetic.
  - "I slowly changed their mind and they started dressing like me. This is my vibe."
  - Customers and attendees at Vercel events now dress in black. "It means a lot."
