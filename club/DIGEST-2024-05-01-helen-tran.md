## Summary

Helen Tran (design leader at AngelList; ex-Shopify Toronto product-design growth 1→30; founder of Jupiter, beauty-ops software ~3y) on how three years as a founder rewired her design practice and leadership. Arc: Canadian college diploma → graphic design → photography/fashion → UX agency acquired into Shopify (278→~4,000) → Jupiter → AngelList (~6 months in at recording). Core claims: founder years teach why things aren't priority and keep design tied to the bottom line; workplace politics/emotion-storms are usually products of instability — people are happiest when they're winning; better thinking = pattern recognition across revenue, PMF, philosophy, and qualitative data, not pure craft dogma; applied scientific process (Notion taxonomy of disparate signals → trends → hypothesis) beats AI summary for early insight; ask *why* like a toddler until you confirm what you knew or uncover the unexpected (COGS-by-the-milliliter); pre-PMF designers should be the founder's "ideas guy" / mini-founder (pitch, customers, prototypes); career tradeoff — she sacrificed deeper code/motion production skills for MBA/business fluency and now thrives on small teams validating unclear ideas, not scaling mega-orgs; AngelList hires for independence, unlimited agency, and intellectual depth on meaty financial/compliance problems (no cookie-cutter dog-walking apps). Why it matters: founder-mindset / design-leadership episode — **not** Inflight-shaped. She explicitly traded away code prototyping time; hypothesis checks are same-hour **Figma** yes/no with customers, not Slack/live-preview coded-prototype review culture.
