## Summary

Raphael Schaad (founder of Cron → Notion Calendar; MIT Media Lab; design-founder-engineer) on building a calendar startup from fragile sketchbook ideas through YC, Product Hunt Product of the Year, and acquisition. Arc: 2017 observation that people love their calendar but hate calendar software → 2018 AI-powered to-do prototypes (Wizard of Oz) fail user tests / tech not ready → Chrome extension fixing one Google Calendar annoyance explodes overnight (~10k users) and validates the **problem** → YC W20 solo → **Cron Classic**: Electron wrapping calendar.google.com so he can innovate on the 1% (availability sharing drawn on the calendar, command palette, dark mode, rich notifications) while standing on Google's 99% → charge $10/mo to validate willingness-to-pay, then **turn monetization off** to focus execution on a ground-up calendar → paper sketches for fragile ideas, then **sketching in code** (inline HTML/CSS/JS folders, Option-drag copies) because temporal/interaction can't live on paper or static Figma — often **jumps the Figma middle** → weekly early-access cohorts (not splash launches) until Bookface then Nov 2021 open → 50/50 reactive feedback vs vision tickets; COVID elevates conferencing/menu-bar join, defers rooms → deliberately underinvested one-button landing page → campsite / broken-windows entropy fight as team quality bar (Swiss watch) → dialogues & toasts as the app's conversation with the user; gigantic Figma permutation files kept in sync via PR-template Slack pings → **design toolkit** (lightweight) vs big-company **design system** → fast + high craft as the apprentice lesson → Notion Calendar launch: multiproduct Notion, 1M+ signups day one → Config talk teaser on the power of paper. Core claims: validate the MVP of the *problem* before the solution; innovate on the 1% atop giants; interaction *is* the design so explore in working code ("sticks and stones"); good design includes how conversational UI is built and worded in the codebase; fight entropy so quality survives a team.
